# Blood Donor Eligibility Rules (informational, not medical advice)

These rules are an editable knowledge base used by the eligibility chatbot.
They are drawn from common guidance published by the WHO, the American Red
Cross, and the National Blood Transfusion Service of India. Always defer to
the medical staff at the blood bank for final eligibility.

---

## Age and weight

- Minimum age for whole-blood donation is 18 years. Some countries allow 17 with parental consent.
- Maximum age for first-time donors is typically 60 years; repeat donors can often donate until 65.
- Minimum body weight is 50 kilograms (110 pounds).
- Donors must be in generally good health on the day of donation.

## Frequency between donations

- Whole-blood donors must wait at least 56 days (8 weeks) between donations.
- Double red-cell (Power Red) donors must wait at least 112 days (16 weeks).
- Platelet (apheresis) donors can donate as often as every 7 days, up to 24 times per year.
- Plasma-only (plasmapheresis) donors can donate as often as every 28 days.

## Hemoglobin and vitals

- Hemoglobin must be at least 12.5 g/dL for women and 13.0 g/dL for men.
- Blood pressure must be between 90/50 mmHg and 180/100 mmHg at the time of donation.
- Pulse must be between 50 and 100 beats per minute (athletes with lower resting pulse may still qualify).
- Body temperature must be 37.5 C (99.5 F) or less.

## Recent illness, medication, and procedures

- Defer donation for at least 14 days after recovery from a cold, flu, or fever.
- After taking antibiotics for a bacterial infection, defer for 7 days after the final dose.
- After a COVID-19 infection, defer for at least 14 days after symptoms resolve.
- Most common medications (blood pressure, statins, allergy meds, oral contraceptives, hormone replacement) do NOT defer donation.
- Aspirin and ibuprofen do NOT defer whole-blood donation but do defer platelet donation by 48 hours.
- Isotretinoin (Accutane) requires a 1-month deferral after the last dose.
- Finasteride / dutasteride / hair-loss medications require a 1-month deferral after the last dose.

## Tattoos, piercings, and acupuncture

- Tattoos done at a state-regulated parlor with sterile single-use equipment: NO deferral in most US states; otherwise defer 3 to 6 months depending on local rules.
- Body piercings with sterile single-use equipment: NO deferral; otherwise defer 3 to 6 months.
- Acupuncture by a licensed practitioner with sterile needles: NO deferral.

## Travel

- After travel to a malaria-endemic country, defer donation for 3 months.
- After living in a malaria-endemic country for more than 5 years, defer for 3 years after departure.
- After travel to a Zika-affected area, defer for 4 weeks.

## Pregnancy and breastfeeding

- Defer donation for 6 weeks after giving birth.
- Pregnant donors are not eligible.
- Breastfeeding mothers may donate if the baby is older than 9 months and they are well-nourished, though many programs prefer to wait until weaning.

## Surgery and dental work

- After major surgery, defer for 6 months and only resume once fully recovered.
- After minor outpatient surgery, defer for 7 days.
- After routine dental cleaning, defer for 24 hours.
- After a tooth extraction or root canal, defer for 72 hours.

## Vaccinations

- Inactivated vaccines (flu shot, COVID-19 mRNA vaccines, tetanus, hepatitis A/B): NO deferral.
- Live-attenuated vaccines (MMR, varicella, yellow fever, nasal flu): defer for 4 weeks.
- Smallpox vaccine: defer for 8 weeks.

## Permanent deferrals (do NOT donate)

- A confirmed positive test for HIV, HTLV, or hepatitis B/C.
- A history of Creutzfeldt-Jakob disease, or a family history of variant CJD.
- A history of receiving a dura mater (brain covering) graft.
- A history of injecting non-prescription drugs.
- A history of babesiosis or Chagas disease.

## On the day of donation

- Eat a healthy iron-rich meal in the 3 hours before donation.
- Drink at least 500 mL of water in the hour before donating.
- Avoid heavy lifting or strenuous exercise for 24 hours after donating.
- Keep the bandage on for at least 4 hours and the donation site dry.
- If you feel light-headed afterward, sit or lie down with feet elevated.

## What to bring

- A government-issued photo ID.
- A list of any medications you currently take.
- Your donor card if you are a repeat donor.

## Why donate?

- One whole-blood donation can save up to 3 lives.
- Less than 10% of the eligible population donates blood in most countries.
- The body replaces the plasma lost during donation within 24 hours and the red cells within 4 to 6 weeks.
